# Fire Without Matches — Lens, Battery and Steel Wool, Electronic Parts

## Purpose
When you have no matches or lighter, use alternative materials on hand to produce a spark or focused heat.

## Safety Warning
A short-circuited battery generates heat and can burn your hand — handle steel wool carefully, sparks can fly. Deliberately damaging a phone battery to force a short circuit is NOT recommended — battery damage carries a fire/explosion risk.

## Steps
1. **Lens method:** Eyeglass lenses, binocular/camera lenses, or even a clear water-filled bottle/bag can focus sunlight onto tinder. Hold the smallest, brightest point of light steady on the tinder — smoke should appear within seconds.
2. **Battery + steel wool:** Touching a 9V battery (or the joined ends of two batteries) to steel wool (a ball of fine steel threads) short-circuits the thin strands, making them glow and ignite. Transfer the glow to your tinder immediately.
3. As soon as you get a spark or glow, transfer it into your prepared tinder nest and blow gently to bring it to flame.

## When to Be Careful
The lens method only works in direct sunlight; the battery method drains battery power quickly and may not succeed on the first try — keep a few spare strands of steel wool on hand.

## Reference
General bushcraft knowledge on "modern fire-starting methods" — the lens and battery/steel-wool techniques are widely taught in survival training.
