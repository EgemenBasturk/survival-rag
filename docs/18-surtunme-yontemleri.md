# Friction Methods — Fire Plough and Bow Drill

## Purpose
Knowing how to start a fire using friction heat when you have no tools at all. This is the hardest method, requiring patience and correct technique.

## Safety Warning
Prolonged friction can cause hand/wrist fatigue and friction burns; take frequent breaks.

## Steps
1. **Material selection:** Choose a soft, dry wooden base and a harder rubbing stick — both must be completely dry.
2. **Fire plough:** Cut a straight groove into the base wood, and rub a hard stick back and forth along the groove quickly and with pressure. Hot wood dust from the friction collects at the end of the groove.
3. **Bow drill:** Make a bow from a flexible branch, wrap a cord (like a shoelace) around a spindle, and set it into a small pre-drilled hole in the base. Move the bow back and forth to spin the spindle rapidly, causing hot dust to build up in the base.
4. Carefully transfer the accumulated hot dust into a pre-prepared tinder nest.
5. Blow gently on the tinder nest to bring it to flame — this step usually takes the most practice.

## When to Be Careful
These methods can take hours of practice — if you have another way to start a fire (lens, battery), try that first, and save friction as a last resort.

## Reference
General bushcraft friction-fire techniques — the fire plough and bow drill are standard methods taught in wilderness survival training.
