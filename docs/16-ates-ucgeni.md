# The Fire Triangle and Preparation — Tinder, Kindling, Fuel

## Purpose
Understanding why the right material preparation, before you even try to light a fire, determines success or failure.

## Safety Warning
Don't build a fire right next to your shelter or in the middle of dry vegetation — uncontrolled spread risk. Clear the area around the fire.

## Steps
1. **Tinder:** The finest, driest material, most ready to catch a flame — dry grass, bark fibre, fine shavings. Prepare a full handful.
2. **Kindling:** Sticks slightly thinner than a pencil — once the tinder catches, this grows the flame. Prepare several groups of different thicknesses.
3. **Fuel (main wood):** Wrist-thick branches and above — added once the fire is established, keeps it going.
4. Prepare all your material BEFORE lighting the fire, within reach and in a dry spot — searching for material after the flame has caught is the most common cause of failure.
5. Loosely gather the tinder into a nest shape (leave gaps for air), and stack the thinnest kindling over it in a pyramid or teepee shape.

## When to Be Careful
Piling too much material at once cuts off airflow and smothers the fire — building it up gradually and loosely works better.

## Reference
A general bushcraft fire-starting principle — the fire triangle (tinder-kindling-fuel) is a standard concept in survival training.
