# Signaling with a Mirror or Reflective Surface

## Purpose
One of the most effective, energy-free ways to attract the attention of a distant ship or plane on a sunny day.

## Safety Warning
Never aim the reflected light directly at your own eyes or anyone else's nearby — it can cause temporary vision impairment.

## Steps
1. Any reflective surface can be used: a pocket mirror, a phone's rear camera glass/screen, a can lid, or any shiny piece of metal.
2. Hold the reflector toward the sun, and with your free hand, make a V shape with two fingers pointed at the target (ship/plane).
3. Look at the target through the gap between your fingers, and adjust the reflector so the sunlight's reflection passes through that point.
4. Tilt the reflector slightly to make the light "flash" over the target — a moving, flickering flash draws more attention than a steady glare.
5. Keep repeating as long as the target is visible, especially when you think it's looking in your direction.

## When to Be Careful
This method doesn't work on a cloudy day or if the sun is behind you — you need the sun behind you and the target in front, at the right angle.

## Reference
A general rescue signaling technique — mirror/heliograph signaling is a standard method taught in maritime and aviation rescue training.
